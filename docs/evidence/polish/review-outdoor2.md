# Outdoor review 2 (fresh eyes)

## Scores

**(a) Looks effortful: 7/10**
- The pad hero shot now has a lander with a glowing hatch, struts, pad markings, lamp posts, barcoded crates, drums and tyre tracks. The hazard-striped door is still the best-finished object.
- The science shot has textured grey boulders and a kneeling astronaut. The faceted brown foreground rocks are still bare, flat-shaded polygons, and the lander hull is a plain cone with faint panel lines.
- The pad building from round 1 is gone from the hero frame. The scene is sparser but cleaner.

**(b) Consistent style: 6/10**
- The grey textured boulders look like a different asset family from the flat low-poly brown rocks sitting next to them in the same frame.
- The lander, drums, lamps and astronaut share a chunky low-poly style that holds together. The solar panels and the antenna tower are thin and more detailed, and the antenna stands in front of an unrelated rocket.
- The UI is consistent: dark pills, one sans family, agent colours.

**(c) Readable text at ~1200 px per eye: 7/10**
- Nametags are much bigger ("Cargo Keeper - Receiver", "Science Officer Okonkwo - Science"). Captions and decision cards are large and high contrast.
- The scientist's speech bubble is still small. Its tiny header and two lines are marginal in Cardboard.
- The "Engineer - Requester - tap to ask" tag is OK in solar. The bubble in the older storm frame is mush. The decision-card text is clear, but a truncated "(exp..." is visible.

**(d) Believable Mars: 7/10**
- Shadows and the ground detail texture help a lot. Pad and science look grounded, with horizon ridges and haze.
- The storm frame reads as a wall of brown with streaks and a darkened ground. It is convincing but shows no distinct front on the horizon.
- The sky is a flat, empty gradient with no sun disc or glow, and the ground colour is uniform. Close-up pebbles are sparse.

**(e) Story clarity: 7/10**
- The lander is now central, with an open lit hatch and crates at its feet, so "just landed" works. It lacks scorch or a heat shimmer.
- The scientist sampling beside a flag reads clearly. A sample bag or core tube would sell the action.
- Storm to door: the dust, darkening and caption work, then a straight cut to the door. The door close-up is too tight, so there is no sense of walking to it. The antenna tower beside the rocket makes the second rocket (outdoor-solar) confusing.

## Previous five fixes

## Next five fixes
1. Enlarge the speech bubbles about 2x with a bigger name header, and do not truncate text.
2. Give the brown faceted rocks the same grey-brown grit texture as the boulders, or tint and swap them, to stop the two rock families clashing.
3. Add a dark scorch decal and a faint dust ring on the ground under the lander, and give the hull a dust-streak texture.
4. Paint a low dark storm band with a ragged top edge on the horizon sky texture, thickening as tau rises, so the storm visibly approaches.
5. Pull the door camera back about 2 m, with a warm glow spill and a lit footpath from the pad, so the "go inside" destination is clear.

## Leave alone
The airlock door detail, the sky colour grade, the caption and card typography, and the shadow and texture pass, which is working.
